## SECTION 1 — THE PROBLEM (2–3 minutes)

### What to say:

"The problem with AI in education isn't that students use it. It's that every AI tool available today makes it trivially easy to bypass learning entirely.

A student struggling with an assignment can paste the prompt into ChatGPT, get a polished finished product, and submit it — without understanding a single thing. The AI did the thinking. The student did the copying.

That's not an AI problem. That's a design problem. These tools were built to answer, not to teach.

And here's what makes it worse: every session starts blank. ChatGPT doesn't know you. It doesn't know what you've already studied, what you've struggled with, what projects you've built. You're a stranger every single time.

So we have AI that's extraordinarily capable, completely amnesiac, and structurally designed to do the work for you. That's the gap I wanted to close."

### If a judge asks: "But can't students just not use AI?"
> "They will use it — that's the reality. The question is whether we design tools that make misuse easy, or tools where the design itself encourages genuine engagement. ARIA is an answer to that second question."

## SECTION 2 — THE SOLUTION: WHAT IS ARIA (2–3 minutes)

### What to say:

"ARIA stands for Adaptive Responsive Intelligent Agent. She is a real-time AI study companion — not a chatbot in a browser tab, but a live animated voice presence that listens, thinks, and responds.

She has three things that no other AI study tool has combined:

**First — she remembers you.** Every conversation, every project you've worked through, every concept you've struggled with — all of it is stored in a memory vault. Not just from sessions with ARIA. You can import your entire conversation history from ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini — every AI you've ever used. ARIA inherits all of it. She knows your full academic history.

**Second — she guides, she doesn't answer.** If you ask ARIA to write your machine learning assignment, she won't. She'll ask: 'okay, what's the problem asking you to do? tell me what you understand so far.' The interaction becomes a conversation, not a transaction. You have to engage. The output of a session with ARIA is understanding — not text you can submit.

**Third — she's emotionally present.** She has a face, a voice, expressions that change in real time based on what she's feeling. She gets excited when something is genuinely interesting. She gets frustrated when you give up too easily. She notices when your energy drops and adjusts. That's not cosmetic. Students learn better when they feel supported, when the interaction feels personal, when they sense that the entity they're talking to actually cares whether they understand."

### The one-sentence version (if asked to summarize):
> "ARIA is an emotionally intelligent AI study companion with long-term memory that guides students toward their own answers instead of providing them."

## SECTION 3 — HOW IT WORKS (2–3 minutes, technical)

### What to say:

"Let me walk you through what happens every time you say something to ARIA.

You speak. A speech-to-text model transcribes your voice in about 350 milliseconds — that's under half a second.

That text goes to a memory proxy I built in Python. Before ARIA ever thinks of a response, the proxy searches a knowledge vault of every conversation ARIA has ever had with you — using a ranking algorithm called BM25. If you asked about regression three weeks ago, that memory is retrieved in milliseconds and injected into the context for this response.

The enriched request then goes to a large language model on Groq's cloud API. The LLM sees your message, your memory, your current date — and generates a response that feels like it comes from someone who actually knows you.

That response streams back token by token. As it arrives, the emotion system reads it in real time. ARIA's personality has her embed emotion tags inline — a thinking face here, a laugh there. Those tags are stripped before text-to-speech so her voice never reads them aloud. Instead, they fire Live2D animations in sync with her audio.

Her voice is generated by ElevenLabs with a custom voice profile. While audio plays, an NVIDIA neural network called Audio2Face reads the audio waveform frame by frame and generates lip-sync blendshapes — so her mouth moves in perfect sync with every word.

Total end-to-end latency from when you finish speaking to when ARIA starts responding: about four to five seconds."

### Architecture summary (point to the screen / whiteboard if possible):
```
You speak → STT (~350ms) → Memory Proxy (BM25 search + inject) 
→ Groq LLM (~3,000ms) → Emotion processor → TTS voice 
→ Audio2Face lip sync → Live2D animated avatar
```

## SECTION 4 — THE MEMORY SYSTEM (1–2 minutes)

### What to say:

"The memory system is what makes ARIA meaningfully different from any AI that resets between sessions.

Every substantial conversation turn is saved as a markdown note — timestamped, topic-tagged, searchable. When you talk to ARIA, she searches those notes in real time and pulls in whatever's relevant.

She doesn't announce it. She doesn't say 'I remember you mentioned...' She just knows it, the way a close friend knows things about you without having to preface every sentence with a citation.

And because you can import conversation exports from any AI platform — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini — ARIA inherits the full academic history of the student, not just the conversations she participated in directly.

A freshman could start using ARIA, import everything they've discussed with AI for the past four years, and ARIA would know their entire learning journey. That's institutional memory of a student, carried forward across every semester."

### What to say:

"ARIA is a demonstration that AI can be designed with a fundamentally different objective — not to answer questions, but to support the human process of learning.

The technical contribution is the integration: real-time voice, long-term memory with cross-platform import, emotion-driven avatar animation, and a pedagogical personality all working together in a single live system.

## LIKELY JUDGE QUESTIONS — AND HOW TO ANSWER THEM

---

**Q: How is this different from just using ChatGPT with a custom system prompt?**

> "Three things ChatGPT doesn't have: persistent long-term memory across sessions, a live animated avatar that expresses emotion in real time, and voice input/output that makes it feel like a real conversation rather than a text interface. And the memory isn't just session history — it's a searchable vault that grows over months and years and can import from any AI platform. That's architecturally different from prompt engineering."

---

**Q: Can't a determined student just use ChatGPT anyway?**

> "Yes. ARIA isn't a control mechanism — it's an alternative that's more engaging to interact with honestly than to misuse. The interface rewards genuine conversation. And because ARIA knows your history, her responses are personalized to you — there's no generic essay to copy. The output is understanding, not text."

---

**Q: What's the latency? Is it usable?**

> "End-to-end is about four to five seconds from when you stop speaking to when ARIA starts responding. That's on par with a natural thinking pause in a real conversation. Users don't notice it as a technical delay — they notice it as ARIA thinking."

---

**Q: What model is powering the LLM?**

> "Llama-4-Scout running on Groq's cloud inference API. The full request pipeline — memory injection, history trimming, rate limiting, retry logic — runs through a custom Python proxy I built that sits between the engine and Groq."

---

**Q: How does the emotion system work?**

> "The LLM is instructed to embed emotion tags inline with its response — like a stage direction embedded in speech. As tokens stream in, a processor extracts those tags before they reach text-to-speech, so her voice never reads them aloud. Instead they're timestamped and used to trigger Live2D expressions in sync with the audio. Seventeen emotion states, all mapped to the avatar's animation rig."

---

**Q: What would you build next?**

> "The most impactful next step is enabling adaptive difficulty — where ARIA tracks which concepts a student has mastered across sessions and dynamically calibrates how much support she gives. Right now she's context-aware but not yet learning-progress-aware in a structured sense. The memory system is already the foundation for that."
